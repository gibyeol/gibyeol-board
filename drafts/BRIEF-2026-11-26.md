## Why this add-on
The owner's main complaint (2026-11-26): every page is only text. Readers get tired because they must read everything to understand. Each page must be understandable at a glance through visuals, while keeping the checked-facts rules of BRIEF.md.

## Visual rules (required)
1. **At-a-glance first.** Right after the front matter, the "one-line judgment" table (BRIEF principle 3), max 4 rows.
2. **Procedures as steps.** Any how-to (buy, tap, transfer, refund) is a numbered list `1. ...` — the site renders numbered lists as step cards. Keep each step to one or two short sentences.
3. **Figures by need, not by count** (owner, 2026-11-26 01:23: "그림의 기준은 그림의 개수 같은걸로 정하는게 아니라 "그림이 필요한가"로 가야돼. 외국인이 보기에 버스, 지하철, 등등 낯선 환경에 대한 이해력이 부족하기 때문에 이해를 위해서 그림을 무조건 넣어줘야하고, 제휴관련된 부분도 후킹을 위해서는 그림이 무조건 들어가야하지 않겠어?"). For every `##` section ask: would a first-time foreign visitor understand this faster with a picture? Required: (a) anything about an unfamiliar Korean environment or object — buses, subway, ticket machines, stations, signs, receipts, forms, counters, kiosks, apps, store layouts; (b) every section that carries an affiliate button or recommends a product/service (a figure that shows what the reader gets or how it works — drawn, no copied product photos or logos). A section that is only a short judgment or a source note does not need one. Place each figure in the section it explains, shortcode on its own line:
   `{{fig:<slug>-<n>|Caption in that language}}`
   For each figure you write one SVG per language: `/home/user/gibyeol-board/static/fig/<lang>/<slug>-<n>.svg` (lang = en, ja, zh-tw, ko). The build stops if a referenced file is missing.
   Good figure types: a step flow with simple icons, a fare/price comparison bar or table-card, a map-like schematic (not a real map copy), a "what to look for" annotated mock-up (e.g. a ticket machine or receipt drawn with simple shapes), a decision tree ("Are you staying 3+ days? → ...").
4. **SVG rules:** hand-written SVG, `viewBox` set, no fixed width/height attributes (the page scales it), phone-first: viewBox width 400 (tall/vertical layouts fine), min font-size 16 in that viewBox, so text stays ≥13.6px on a 340px-wide phone column (15 would give 12.75px) (changed 2026-11-26 01:09 after a phone screenshot showed 720-wide figures shrink text to ~6px), font-family `system-ui, -apple-system, "Noto Sans KR", "Noto Sans JP", "Noto Sans TC", sans-serif`. Palette: ink #16151a, red #b8392f (one accent), paper #f4f0e6, muted #5f5b55, rule #c9c1b2, white #ffffff, plus one soft fill #e9e2d3. No external images, no fonts loaded from the web, no real brand logos or copied photos/maps (draw neutral shapes; a brand name in plain text is fine). Text inside the SVG must be in that SVG's language and must match the facts in the page (no new facts in figures). Keep text short (labels, not paragraphs).
5. **Short sections.** Each `##` section: one judgment sentence, then the steps/figure/table. Aim 600–1,100 words per edition.
6. Callouts: a line starting with `> ` becomes a highlighted box — use for one warning or one tip per section at most.

## The Nadri principles (top standard — added 2026-11-26 after the checklist comparison found this brief never cited them)
Source: Nadri 기초 원칙서 rev 11 (read-only copy: gibyeol/wondu-archive docs/원칙서-current.md, part 4). "원칙 10개를 통과하지 못한 글은 올리지 않는다." If a rule in this brief conflicts with the principles, the principles win.

| # | Principle | Check question (answer yes/no for your page) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show what they will like, don't only make them want | Is there a reason the reader will still like it a week after buying/doing it? |
| 2 | Facts with sources, judgments in our voice | Does every number, date and rule have a source you opened (with a link)? Is there at least one judgment? |
| 3 | Stage a day, not an object | Can the reader see the scene of using it or going there? Did you choose an ending scene? |
| 4 | Give them the waiting | Is there something to savour before departure (a preview, the pleasure of preparing)? |
| 5 | Carry only real meaning | Is every attached story (place, craftsperson, history) confirmed? |
| 6 | Pick three and show why | Is the choosing rule visible instead of a list? Did you say what you left out? |
| 7 | Exact expectations, surprises on site | No inflated adjectives or superlatives? |
| 8 | Only devices that still work when explained | Could we explain every device on the page to the reader? No fake urgency/scarcity/reviews? Affiliate disclosed? |
| 9 | Look down on no one | No looking down on people, countries or tastes, no "you must live like this" pressure? |
| 10 | Only what's worth browsing | Would someone it doesn't concern still want to look at this page? Is it distinct from other pages out there? |

How-to pages are not exempt. The principles document's own example (part 5, airport guide): stage "your first hour in Korea" — the view changing outside the window, the beep of your first T-money tap, one scene you can see right after arriving — and put the comparison table under it. Build scenes only from confirmed facts (no invented sensations presented as fact; a clearly marked "our call"/"what you'll notice" built from checked facts is fine). No invented local habits ("what locals do") without a source.

**Required in every writer report:** a table of the 10 principles with yes/partly/no and the line in the page that answers each.
