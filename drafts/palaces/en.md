## The short answer

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Only time for one palace | Gyeongbokgung, from opening at 9:00 | The biggest palace, with a free guard-changing ceremony at the gate at 10:00 |
| It's a Tuesday | Changdeokgung or Deoksugung instead | Gyeongbokgung and Jongmyo close on Tuesdays; the other three close on Mondays |
| You want the quiet, green one | Book Changdeokgung's Secret Garden (후원) | Guided, 100 people per session, and half the places go online first |
| Visiting three or more sites | Buy the 6,000 won integrated ticket | Five sites for less than paying separately, valid six months |
| Thinking of renting hanbok | Do it for the photos, not the free ticket | You save 1,000–3,000 won at the gate; the rental costs far more |
| Bukchon Hanok Village | Go between 10:00 and 17:00 | Outside those hours tourists are barred from the core lanes, with a 100,000 won fine |

## If you only see one palace

Our call is **Gyeongbokgung**. Arrive at 9:00, watch the guard-changing ceremony in front of Gwanghwamun gate at 10:00, then spend the rest of the morning inside. The ceremony is free and you don't need a palace ticket to watch it. It runs at 10:00 and 14:00 for about 20 minutes, with shorter gate-guard rituals at 11:00 and 13:00. There is none on Tuesdays, when the palace is closed, and bad weather can cancel it.

Pick **Changdeokgung** instead if Gyeongbokgung is closed (Tuesday), if you would rather have fewer crowds and more garden, or if you managed to book the Secret Garden.

**Who can skip palaces:** if you have already seen one palace on an earlier trip, the others look similar from the outside. Changdeokgung's Secret Garden is the one that feels genuinely different.

## Closing days and hours

Every day of the week at least some palaces are open, because the closing days are split:

| Site | Closed |
|---|---|
| Gyeongbokgung, Jongmyo | Tuesday |
| Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung, Deoksugung | Monday |

If a closing day falls on a public holiday, the palace opens and closes on the next non-holiday day instead. This is in the Korea Heritage Service's viewing regulations (amended 5 February 2026). For how that plays out over Seollal 2027, see [Seoul in winter](/winter/).

Palaces open at 9:00. They close at 18:00 most of the year, 18:30 from June to August, and **17:30 from November to January**. Last entry is an hour before closing, so in winter be through the gate by 16:30.

## What it costs

| Ticket | Adult price |
|---|---|
| Gyeongbokgung | 3,000 won |
| Changdeokgung (palace grounds) | 3,000 won |
| Changdeokgung Secret Garden tour | 5,000 won extra |
| Changgyeonggung, Deoksugung, Jongmyo | 1,000 won each |
| Integrated ticket (4 palaces + Jongmyo, one visit each, valid 6 months) | 6,000 won |

**Free for foreign visitors aged 18 or under or 65 and over**, and for anyone in hanbok (see below).

The integrated ticket does not include the Secret Garden. It pays off only if your plan includes both Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung plus one more site. At Gyeongbokgung it is sold at the booth in front of Heungnyemun gate.

**Prices may change.** These fees have not moved since 2005. The Korea Heritage Service planned a rise from January 2027, but as of 8 October 2026 (Yonhap) the timing was "undecided" and the agency said it would improve visitor services first. One proposal on the table was 5,000 won for palaces and Jongmyo. We will update when an official notice is out.

**Free Wednesdays.** In May 2026 the Heritage Service announced free entry every Wednesday at Deoksugung from August and at Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo from October (not Gyeongbokgung, not the Secret Garden). We could not confirm on the official site that this is running now, so check [royal.khs.go.kr](https://royal.khs.go.kr) before you count on it.

## Hanbok: is renting worth it?

Wearing hanbok gets you into the palaces free. The rules are specific: you need a top (jeogori) and a skirt or trousers, ties and buttons done up, and legs covered. A jeogori with jeans, a skirt with a T-shirt, or T-shirt-style "hanbok" do not count. Modern-style hanbok is fine.

**For:** the photos are the point, and the palaces are the backdrop people come for. Free entry is a small bonus.

**Against:**
- The saving is 1,000–3,000 won. If money is your reason, it isn't one.
- It eats time: fitting, then returning it by a set hour, so you can't drift on to Bukchon or dinner.
- Long skirts and borrowed shoes on gravel and stairs are tiring for a full day.
- From November to January you will be cold. The ties have to stay fastened, so plan the layers underneath.

**Our call:** rent for two or three hours around one palace if photos matter to you. Skip it if you want to see several sites in one day. Also note the free-entry rule is under review: in October 2026 the agency said it would look at the hanbok policy as part of the fee review.

## The Secret Garden (Changdeokgung 후원)

This is the one palace experience worth booking ahead. It is a guided walk of about 70–90 minutes through the garden behind Changdeokgung, with 100 people per session. Fifty places are sold online from 10:00 six days before the visit date, first come first served; the other fifty are sold on site that day. Korean sessions are most frequent, English sessions are also common, and Japanese and Chinese sessions are limited. Book through the official palace site. In spring and autumn the on-site queue can be long, so book online if you can.

## Jongmyo: check the day

Jongmyo, the royal ancestral shrine, works differently. On weekdays (it is closed Tuesdays) you can only go in on a guided tour at set times, about 50 minutes. On weekends and public holidays you can walk around on your own. If you want to wander at your own pace, go on a weekend.

## Bukchon: go between 10 and 5

Bukchon Hanok Village is a neighbourhood where people live, not a theme park. Since 1 March 2025 the busiest part, around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, is closed to tourists from 17:00 to 10:00. Breaking the rule can cost a **100,000 won fine**, after a warning. Residents, their relatives, and people visiting shops and businesses there are exempt. Large tour buses are also banned from a 2.3 km stretch of the area.

What locals would tell you: keep your voice down, don't open gates or sit on doorsteps, and take your rubbish with you. Bukchon sits between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung, so the easy plan is palace in the morning, Bukchon around midday, second palace in the afternoon.

## Rainy day or evening: National Museum of Korea

The [National Museum of Korea](https://www.museum.go.kr) is free for the permanent galleries and stays open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays (other days 9:30–17:30). It closes on 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok, and the permanent galleries also close on the first Monday of March, June, September and December, which this year is **Monday 7 December 2026**. If it rains on palace day, swap.
