## Our call, in one table

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You want sunscreen | Match SPF/PA to your day, not the biggest number | Korea's food and drug regulator (MFDS) says SPF15/PA+ is enough for indoors and short walks |
| You want brightening or wrinkle care | Buy only if the box says **기능성화장품** and names that function | That claim was reviewed by or reported to MFDS; "glow" on the front was not |
| Fragrance bothers your skin | Read the ingredient list for 향료 and allergen names | Above a set level, fragrance allergens must be named one by one |
| The ad says "cell regeneration", "anti-inflammatory" or "Botox" | Put it back | MFDS calls these illegal claims for cosmetics |

## Read the label in 30 seconds

You only need to find three things on the back.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-1|What to look for on the back of a Korean cosmetics box (example, no real brand)}}

1. **Find 기능성화장품** ("functional cosmetic"), as words or the official symbol. Korean law requires one of them on functional products.
2. **Read the efficacy line (효능·효과).** It names the function reviewed by or reported to MFDS, such as whitening (미백), wrinkle improvement (주름개선) or UV protection (자외선 차단).
3. **Scan 전성분** (full ingredients) for 향료 (fragrance) and named allergens if your skin reacts to fragrance.
4. **Check the volume** if you will carry it on the plane (see the last section).

> No 기능성화장품 on the box means the brightening or anti-wrinkle wording is marketing, not a reviewed claim. MFDS's own advice: check that a product is a functional cosmetic reported to MFDS before you buy.

## Sunscreen: read SPF and PA, then stop chasing numbers

Higher is not automatically better for a city day. MFDS matches the number to what you are doing.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-3|SPF is UVB, PA is UVA; MFDS suggests matching them to your day}}

- **SPF** is the UVB protection index, printed as a whole number; 50 or higher is written **SPF50+**.
- **PA** is the UVA protection grade: PA+, PA++, PA+++, PA++++. More plus signs mean a higher grade.
- MFDS: SPF15/PA+ or more for indoors or a short walk; SPF50+ with PA+++ or PA++++ for long hours in strong sun, such as hiking or the beach.

Our call: buy for the trip you are actually taking, because MFDS ties the level of protection to the activity. A palace walk in December and a summer day on a Busan beach are not the same purchase.

## Choose by what you want it to do

Start from the job, not the shelf.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-2|Pick by function: the label check that goes with each goal}}

### If fragrance bothers your skin

Fragrance may be listed simply as 향료, but 25 fragrance allergens must be named on their own above a set level: over 0.01% in rinse-off products, over 0.001% in leave-on products. Two on the list are 리모넨 (limonene) and 시트로넬올 (citronellol). If those are your triggers, look for products without them.

> If you have a skin condition or use prescription treatment, ask a doctor or pharmacist before trying something new. This page is about reading labels, not about what any product will do for your skin.

## Treat rankings and testers as starting points

A ranking tells you what sells, not what suits you. We have not seen an official explanation of how Olive Young's rankings are calculated, so read them as a popularity signal only.

1. Use the ranking to shortlist two or three items in your category.
2. Run the label check above on each one.
3. If there is a tester, check texture and smell first.
4. Pick the one that passes the label check and fits your day, even if it is ranked lower.

## Skip the hype

Medical-sounding claims are the clearest red flag. In March 2025 MFDS checked 200 online cosmetics posts and caught 144 breaking the law with claims like "cell regeneration", "anti-inflammatory", "stem cell" and "Botox you apply". In August 2024 it caught 82 of 100 microneedle cosmetics posts, including "skin regeneration" and needles said to reach the dermis.

What we would not buy on hype: anything sold as a treatment or promising procedure-like results, and a fourth sunscreen because a new one went viral.

## Getting it home

Plan the suitcase before checkout: toners, gels and mists all count as liquids.

1. **Cabin bag:** each liquid, gel or spray in a container of 100 mL or less, all in one clear 1 L zip bag per person (Incheon Airport, international flights).
2. **Bigger bottles:** checked baggage. Incheon Airport limits personal sprays such as hairspray there to 500 mL per container and 2 L in total.
3. **Duty-free liquids:** keep them in the sealed duty-free liquids bag until you board the flight to your final destination. A transfer airport abroad may apply its own rules.

Check your own airline before you fly; limits can differ.
