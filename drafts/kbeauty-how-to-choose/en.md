## Read the label in 30 seconds

The back of the box is in Korean, but you only need to find three things.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-1|What to look for on the back of a Korean cosmetics box (example, no real brand)}}

1. **Find 기능성화장품** ("functional cosmetic"), as words or a symbol. MFDS tells shoppers to look for it on the package.
2. **Read the line next to it.** It says which function was reviewed, such as whitening (미백), wrinkle improvement (주름개선) or UV protection (자외선 차단).
3. **Scan 전성분** (full ingredients) for 향료 (fragrance) and named allergens if your skin reacts to fragrance.
4. **Check the volume** if you will carry it on the plane (see the last section).

> No 기능성화장품 on the box means the brightening or anti-wrinkle wording is marketing, not a reviewed claim. MFDS says sunscreen, whitening and wrinkle products should be bought only after checking they were reviewed. In Korean, you can also look a product up on MFDS's 의약품안전나라 site.

## Sunscreen: read SPF and PA, then stop chasing numbers

Higher is not automatically better for a city day. MFDS's own guide matches the number to what you are doing.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-3|SPF is UVB, PA is UVA; MFDS suggests matching them to your day}}

- **SPF** is the UVB protection index. Korea prints it as a number up to 50; anything above 50 is written **SPF50+**.
- **PA** is the UVA protection grade: PA+, PA++, PA+++, PA++++. More plus signs mean a higher UVA grade.
- MFDS: SPF15/PA+ or more for indoors or a short walk; SPF50+ with PA+++ or PA++++ for long hours in strong sun, such as hiking or the beach.

What locals do: buy for the trip you are actually taking. A palace walk in December and a summer day on a Busan beach are not the same purchase.

## Choose by what you want it to do

Start from your job, not the shelf.

{{fig:kbeauty-how-to-choose-2|Pick by function: the label check that goes with each goal}}

### If fragrance bothers your skin

Korean labels list every ingredient. Fragrance can appear simply as 향료, but 25 fragrance allergens have to be named on their own once they pass a set level (over 0.01% in rinse-off products, over 0.001% in leave-on products). Two you will see often are 리모넨 (limonene) and 시트로넬롤 (citronellol). Ask the shop for a product without those names if they are your triggers.

> If you have a skin condition or use prescription treatment, ask a doctor or pharmacist before trying something new. This page is about reading labels, not about what any product will do for your skin.

## Treat rankings and testers as starting points

A ranking tells you what sells, not what suits you. Olive Young shows live rankings in its app and stores, but we could not find a public explanation of how they are calculated, so read them as a popularity signal only.

1. Use the ranking to shortlist two or three items in your category.
2. Run the label check above on each one.
3. If a tester is out, check the texture and smell before you buy a full size.
4. Pick the one that passes the label check and fits your day, even if it is ranked lower.

## Skip the hype

Medical-sounding claims are the clearest red flag. MFDS checked 200 online cosmetics posts in March 2025 and found 144 broke the law with claims like cell regeneration, anti-inflammatory and "Botox you apply". In August 2025 it flagged 83 more, including "skin regeneration" and microneedle products said to reach the dermis. MFDS has also said cosmetics cannot contain human stem cells, whatever "stem cell" on a label suggests.

What we would not buy on hype: anything sold as a treatment, anything promising procedure-like results, and a fourth sunscreen because a new one went viral.

## Getting it home

Plan the suitcase before the checkout, because toners, gels and mists all count as liquids.

1. **Cabin bag:** each liquid, gel or spray in a container of 100 mL or less, all in one clear 1 L zip bag per person (Incheon Airport rule for international flights).
2. **Bigger bottles:** put them in checked baggage. Korean Air limits items such as perfume and hairspray to 500 mL per container and 2 L per person in checked bags.
3. **Duty-free liquids:** keep them in the sealed duty-free bag with the receipt, and do not open it until you board your last flight. A transfer airport abroad may apply its own rules.

Check your own airline before you fly; limits can differ.
