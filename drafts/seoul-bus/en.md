## The one-line judgment

| Situation | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your stop is far from a subway station | Take the bus, with a loaded card | Seoul lists 183 bus routes that take no cash |
| Getting off any bus | Tap your card on the reader again | Without that tap the transfer chain breaks |
| Unsure where to get off | Follow the route in Naver Map | It works in English, Japanese and Chinese |
| Out after the last train | An N bus, ₩2,500 | Night routes; check your route's times in the app |

This page is only about Seoul's city buses. The subway is on [Seoul subway](/seoul-subway/); taxis, last trains and which card to buy are on [Getting around Seoul](/getting-around/). Airport to hotel is on [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/airport-to-seoul/). Who does not need this page: if your hotel and sights are all near subway stations, the subway alone is simpler.

## Read the colour first

The colour of a Seoul bus tells you what kind of route it is, so you can tell at a glance if it is the right kind of bus.

{{fig:seoul-bus-1|Seoul bus types and adult card fares}}

- **Blue (trunk)** buses link outer districts with the centre on long routes, ₩1,500.
- **Green (branch)** buses connect neighbourhoods with trunk buses and the subway, ₩1,500. Village buses are green too and charge ₩1,200.
- **Yellow (circular)** buses loop around the centre, ₩1,400.
- **Red (wide-area)** buses link the wider metro area with central Seoul, ₩3,000. The Climate Card short-term pass does **not** cover them.
- **Route numbers starting with N** are night buses, ₩2,500.

All fares are adult fares paid with a transport card.

## How to ride, step by step

Seoul's rule is simple: tap when you get on, tap again when you get off.

1. **Load your card before you go.** A T-money card or another Korean transport card. Which card and where to top up: [Essentials](/essentials/).
2. **Check the route number** in Naver Map or Kakao Map, then match it on the front of the bus.
3. **Tap your card on the reader** as you board.
4. **Watch your stop come up** on the map, and get ready a stop early.
5. **Tap your card on the reader again,** then get off.

{{fig:seoul-bus-2|Tap in when you board, tap out when you get off}}

> Seoul says that if you don't tap off, the transfer chain breaks there, and the base fare you skipped is charged when you board the next bus or subway.

**Cash:** Seoul's January 2026 list names 183 cashless city bus routes, out of 396 city bus routes. On those you can't pay in cash at all. Rather than checking a list route by route, our call is simple: board every bus with a loaded card.

## Transfers: the 30-minute window

A transfer is not a new fare as long as you tapped off and board the next ride within the window.

{{fig:seoul-bus-3|How long you have to transfer after tapping off}}

1. **Tap off** when you leave the first bus.
2. **Board the next bus or subway within 30 minutes** (60 minutes if you board the next ride between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m.).
3. **You pay one base fare for the first 10 km** (the higher base fare of the vehicles you used), plus ₩100 for every 5 km after that. Up to four transfers, five rides in all, count.

Re-boarding the same route or the same bus does not count as a transfer. If a red bus is part of the trip, the base distance is 30 km instead of 10. The wider fare picture is on [Getting around Seoul](/getting-around/).

## Where to get off

Our call: let the app do the watching. Naver Map can be switched to English, Japanese or Chinese and gives bus, subway and walking routes; Kakao Map has an English option. Open the route before you board, keep the screen on, and move towards the door when your stop is next.

## Airport buses are a different thing

City buses and airport limousine buses look like they belong together, but they are separate services.

| | City bus | Airport limousine bus |
|---|---|---|
| Fare (adult) | ₩1,200–3,000 | ₩16,000–18,000 into Seoul |
| Ticket | Tap your card | Ticket from an airport kiosk or bus ticket counter; T-money accepted, but ticket holders board first |
| Climate Card pass | Yes (not red buses) | No |
| Big suitcases | Not what it is for | Staff load up to 2 bags per person (28 in or smaller) |

Watch out for night numbers: the late-night airport buses (N6000, N6002, N6701, N6703) also start with N, but cost ₩17,000–18,000 and you buy a ticket. They are not the ₩2,500 city N buses. Details on [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/airport-to-seoul/).

## Night buses

After the subway stops, Seoul runs night buses whose route numbers start with N, at ₩2,500 for adults with a card. Hours and frequency differ by route, so look your route and the next bus up in Naver Map before you leave, not at the stop.

> We could not confirm the current number of night routes or their hours on an official page, so we don't list them here.

## Common mistakes

These come from Seoul's rules above, not from traveller stories we collected.

1. **Getting off without tapping.** The transfer breaks and the next ride charges the base fare again.
2. **Boarding with no card or an empty one.** Some routes take no cash.
3. **Taking a red bus on a Climate Card pass.** It isn't covered.
4. **Waiting too long between rides.** After 30 minutes (60 at night) the transfer is gone.
5. **Mixing up airport N buses and city N buses.** The fares are about seven times apart.
