## The short answer

| What happened | Do this first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Someone is hurt, a fire, can't breathe | Call **119** | Ambulance and fire. Free, 24 hours |
| Crime, threat, assault, a fight, you feel unsafe | Call **112** | Police, with English and Chinese interpreters 24 hours |
| You need a translator for any of the above | Call **1330**, or ask 112/119 for interpretation | 1330 can join a three-way call with you and emergency services |
| Left a phone or bag in a taxi | Contact the driver through your taxi app, or trace the card you paid with | Fastest route back, before it goes to the police |
| Lost your passport | Police station first, then your embassy | Embassies ask for the police loss report |
| Feeling ill, not an emergency | Pharmacy, or call **1339** | See [Getting sick in Korea](/health/) |

Our call: save 112, 119 and 1330 in your phone before you land, and screenshot the phrase cards at the bottom of this page. Those two things cover almost everything here.

## The numbers, and what each one is for

| Number | For | Language |
|---|---|---|
| 112 | Police | English and Chinese interpreters in-house, 24 hours; other languages through interpreters |
| 119 | Ambulance and fire | Ask for an interpreter, or use 1330 |
| 1330 | Korea Travel Hotline: information, interpretation, complaints | 8 languages, 7 a.m. to midnight |
| 1339 | Health questions and infectious disease (KDCA) | 24 hours |
| 1345 | Immigration Contact Center (visas, stay) | Multilingual |

Before the trip, 1330 is +82-2-1330 from abroad. Details on 1330's chat channels are on our [Everyday Korea](/everyday/) page.

One thing that changed: Korea's dedicated **tourist police** were disbanded in 2023. Don't look for them; call 112. In 2026 the police added foreign-affairs teams of officers chosen for their language skills, starting with Jongno, Yongsan and Jung in Seoul.

## What visitors actually run into

### A phone, wallet or bag left in a taxi

This is the most common one, and usually the most fixable.

- **Booked with an app** (Kakao T and similar): the trip record shows the driver's number. Call or message the driver.
- **Paid by card**: transport-card companies can trace the taxi from your card number. T-money is 1644-1188 and Cashbee is 1644-1472 (Seoul city guidance). Have the card number ready.
- **Paid cash, no app**: note the plate number if you can, the pickup and drop-off place and the time. Ask 1330 to help you call the taxi association.
- Taxi companies keep lost items for about a week before handing them to the police.

### Something left on the subway or a bus

Note the line, the direction, the platform and the time, and tell station staff straight away. Seoul Metro runs lost-and-found centres at City Hall (lines 1–2), Chungmuro (3–4), Wangsimni (5, 8) and Taereung (6–7), weekdays 9:00–18:00, and keeps items for about a week before valuables go to the police (Seoul city guidance, 2023).

Once an item reaches the police, it is kept for six months. Since January 2026 the police search is on the **Police Civil Service 24** site (minwon24.police.go.kr), which replaced LOST112. It asks for Korean phone authentication, so the realistic route for a visitor is: ask your hotel front desk, station staff or 1330 to search for you.

### A lost or stolen passport

1. Go to the nearest police station or police box (지구대 / 파출소) and ask for a **loss report confirmation** (분실신고 확인서). Embassies, immigration offices and banks ask for it.
2. Contact your embassy for an emergency travel document or new passport. Bring passport photos, a copy or the number of the lost passport if you have it, the police report and your flight details. How long it takes depends on your country: the Netherlands, for example, quotes about three working days for an emergency document after the appointment.
3. If your departure date is close, tell the embassy at the first contact. If you have questions about your stay or visa, call 1345.

Our advice: keep a photo of your passport's photo page and a spare passport photo in your bag. Five minutes before the trip saves a day during it.

### An overcharged taxi fare

Seoul received more than 6,000 fare complaints from foreigners in five years, and most were closed without action, mainly because of misunderstood surcharges or no evidence (Seoul city, October 2026). What makes a complaint stick:

- **A receipt.** Ask for 영수증 (yeongsujeung) before you get out, or pay by card.
- **The plate number, the route and the meter fare.** A photo of the meter or the plate is enough.
- **Know the legal extras** so you aren't fighting a real one: late-night surcharges and road tolls are the usual misunderstandings.

Then report through 1330, which handles tourist complaints. Using an app taxi or paying by card makes the whole problem much rarer, because the route and fare are recorded.

### A night that goes wrong

Harassment around bars in Itaewon, Hongdae and Gangnam is the risk foreign governments warn about most. Don't leave drinks unattended, use app taxis late at night, and call 112 if anyone threatens you. More on this in [Everyday Korea](/everyday/).

### Earthquakes, storms and emergency alerts

Korea sends disaster alerts to phones as text messages. The Ministry of Justice recommends the **Emergency Ready App** to foreigners in Korea for disaster safety information such as alerts and shelters. Install it if you are staying more than a few days or travelling in typhoon season.

### Your card stops working

That is usually a block from your bank abroad, not a problem in Korea. Call your bank's international number (on the back of the card), and keep a second card and a little cash for exactly this. Our [Money in Korea](/money/) page has the rest.

## Phrases to show someone nearby

Korean strangers will often help, but many won't feel confident in English. Show the screen. Each card says what you need in plain, polite Korean.

<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">도와주세요. 112(경찰)에 신고해 주시겠어요?</p><p class="say-tr">Please help me. Could you call the police (112) for me?</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">구급차를 불러 주세요. 119에 전화해 주세요.</p><p class="say-tr">Please call an ambulance. Please call 119.</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">한국어를 못 해요. 1330(관광통역안내)에 전화해 주실 수 있나요?</p><p class="say-tr">I don't speak Korean. Could you call 1330, the tourist interpretation line, for me?</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">여권을 잃어버렸어요. 가장 가까운 경찰서(지구대)가 어디예요?</p><p class="say-tr">I lost my passport. Where is the nearest police station?</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">택시에 휴대폰(가방)을 두고 내렸어요. 찾는 것 좀 도와주세요.</p><p class="say-tr">I left my phone (bag) in a taxi. Please help me find it.</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">길을 잃었어요. 이 주소로 가고 싶어요.</p><p class="say-tr">I'm lost. I want to go to this address. (Show the address in Korean.)</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">전화 한 통만 빌려 쓸 수 있을까요?</p><p class="say-tr">Could I borrow your phone for one call?</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">영수증 주세요.</p><p class="say-tr">A receipt, please.</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">근처에 문 연 약국이나 병원이 있나요?</p><p class="say-tr">Is there a pharmacy or clinic open nearby?</p></div>
<div class="say"><p class="say-ko">저는 ___ 알레르기가 있어요.</p><p class="say-tr">I'm allergic to ___. (Write the food or medicine in Korean if you can.)</p></div>

Who doesn't need this page? Nobody, really, but most people never use it. Save the numbers, screenshot the cards, and enjoy the trip.
