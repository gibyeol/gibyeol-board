## The short answer

| Your situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul to Busan and back, few other trains | Buy two regular tickets in the Korail+ app or on letskorail.com | The standard fare is ₩54,400 each way, so a round trip (₩108,800) costs less than the cheapest Korail Pass (₩131,000) |
| Three or more long train rides packed into a few days | Price out the Korail Pass | It only wins when your regular fares add up to more than the pass |
| You want to pay in your own currency | Book on Klook | It has sold live Korail seats since April 2026 |
| Travelling around Seollal (6–9 February 2027) | Book the day holiday sales open, or move your dates | Holiday trains are sold in a separate sale and the busiest days fill fast |

What we left out: sleeper-style tourist trains and regional lines. This page is for the trip most visitors actually make.

## What changed in 2026

Three things, and older guides miss all of them:

- **Booking opens two months ahead.** Since 1 October 2026, KTX and other rail tickets go on sale two months before departure instead of one.
- **KTX and SRT merged.** Since 1 September 2026 they run as one network, and both are booked in one app, Korail+. That app is the old KorailTalk, renamed and updated. If you have KorailTalk on your phone, just update it.
- **Fares went down.** The Seoul–Busan standard fare dropped from ₩59,800 to ₩54,400 on 1 September 2026.

## Where to book as a visitor

- **Korail+ app.** The official app lists English, Japanese, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Vietnamese and Thai. This is where Koreans book, so it shows everything.
- **letskorail.com.** Korail's site for visitors, in English, Japanese and Chinese.
- **Klook.** Since 20 April 2026 it has sold real-time Korail tickets with live seat availability, in about 20 languages and 40 currencies. Handy if your card struggles with Korean checkouts.
- **At the station.** Korail has new ticket machines at 148 stations that take overseas credit cards.

The government says Korail's booking system will grow from 7 to 10 languages by the end of 2026, and that work on WeChat Pay and Alipay is under way. Neither had a launch date when we checked.

## How long it takes

The fastest KTX trains run Seoul to Busan in about 2 hours 17 minutes; most take roughly 2 hours 35 to 41 minutes. Those times come from 2024 reporting and Korail says big time savings won't come before 2028, so expect about the same today. Check the exact time when you book.

## SRT from Suseo: the Gangnam option

SRT trains leave from **Suseo** in southeast Seoul, not Seoul Station. If you are staying in Gangnam or Jamsil, that can save you a cross-town ride. Since the merger, you book them in the same Korail+ app.

One catch: the Korail Pass did not cover SRT as of August 2026, and we could not confirm whether that changed after the merger. If you hold a pass, leave from Seoul or Yongsan unless Korail tells you otherwise.

## Is the Korail Pass worth it?

It is a pass for foreigners only, sold as 2, 3, 4 or 5 travel days. Adult prices reported in December 2025 were ₩131,000, ₩186,000, ₩234,000 and ₩275,000. It covers standard cars on Korail trains, with up to two seat reservations a day. First class costs an extra 50% of the first-class fare. Subway and temporary tourist trains are excluded.

Our call: do the maths before you buy. One Seoul–Busan round trip doesn't reach the price of a 2-day pass. The pass makes sense only if you are covering several long legs in a short window. Prices for youth and group passes are on letskorail.com; we didn't verify them here.

## Seollal 2027

Seollal falls on Sunday 7 February 2027, and the holiday runs 6–9 February. Korean holiday trains are sold in a separate sale; for Seollal 2026 it ran in mid-January. On the busiest day leaving Seoul in 2026, 87.1% of seats sold in that sale. Korail had not announced the 2027 dates when we checked. If you can, travel to Busan before the 6th or after the 9th.

## Landing in Busan instead

Gimhae Airport is Busan's international airport, so you don't have to start in Seoul. From there:

- **Light rail** is the cheapest and fastest way into the city. Change to the Busan Metro at Sasang (Line 2) or Daejeo (Line 3). Busan Station is on Line 1; your map app will show you where to change.
- **Airport Limousine 2** runs to Seomyeon and Bujeon for ₩6,000 (adult), about 50 minutes, every 60–80 minutes.
- **Airport Limousine 1** runs to Haeundae for ₩9,500.
- Limousines are cashless. Buy at the machine on the 1st floor by Gate 4, or tap a card on board if seats are left. Two bags up to 30 kg in total ride free.

## Busan basics

- **Busan Station** is on subway Line 1. Chinatown starts right by Exit 1.
- **Staying in Haeundae?** KTX-Eum trains from Cheongnyangni in eastern Seoul now stop at Sinhaeundae and Centum. They take longer (about 3 hours 38 minutes to Bujeon), but they drop you on the right side of the city.
- **Luggage.** Busan Station has a staffed storage desk on the 1st floor that can also deliver bags to hotels in Haeundae and Gwangalli. We couldn't confirm prices. The city says it is adding self-service storage at the station's Eurasia Platform but gave no date. The Korail+ app also sells luggage delivery as an add-on.
- **Buses.** Since 1 October 2026, Busan has been testing cashless city buses on 35 routes for three months. Carry a transport card (see [essentials](/essentials/)) and you won't notice.
- **Cash and cards:** the same rules apply as in Seoul. See [money](/money/).
