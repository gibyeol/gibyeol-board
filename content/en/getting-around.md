## Your first ride

The first tap is the moment the city opens up: you touch your card to the gate, the reader shows your balance, and you are in. Follow the line number to your platform, change lines without leaving the gates, and tap out at the numbered exit your map app picked. Our pick for the end of a first day: a bus back to the hotel, a second tap as you step down, and no second base fare if your next ride comes within the transfer window.

## The one-line judgment

| Situation | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing the city, before the last train | Subway | Fast, signposted in English, ₩1,550 for the first 10 km |
| Your stop is far from a station | Bus, with a loaded card | Tap off when you get out, or you lose the free transfer |
| After the last train, or with heavy bags | Taxi via k.ride or Kakao T | Foreign cards work in the app; late-night fares are up to 40% higher |
| No transport card yet | Buy one before your first ride | 183 of Seoul's 396 city bus routes take no cash |

{{fig:getting-around-1|Base fares for an adult paying by transport card}}

We picked three ways to move: subway, bus, taxi. What we left out on purpose: Uber, bike share, and intercity trains (KTX has its own page). Airport to hotel: [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/airport-to-seoul/). Step-by-step guides: [Seoul subway](/seoul-subway/) and [Seoul city buses](/seoul-bus/).

## First: get a card

Get a card before your first ride: it is the one problem you cannot fix once you are on a bus.

Subway gates and buses are tap-only. Use T-money, WOWPASS (T-money inside), or a Climate Card short-term pass. Which one and where to top up: [Essentials](/essentials/) and [Money](/money/). What changed in 2026:

- **Since 17 March 2026**, 440 new machines at 273 stations on Seoul Metro Lines 1–8 sell Climate Card passes and single-journey tickets to overseas credit and debit cards, with a 3.7% fee.
- **Tapping your own bank card at the gate still does not work.** Seoul plans to replace gate readers on Lines 1–8 in 2027, then extend to village buses and private rail lines by 2030. Tmoney separately plans to let foreign contactless cards pay on Seoul city buses from 2027.

**Is the Climate Card short-term pass worth it?** Seoul lists 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 days at ₩5,000, ₩8,000, ₩10,000, ₩15,000 and ₩20,000, plus ₩3,000 for the card. So one day costs ₩8,000 with the card, about five subway fares; three days cost ₩13,000, about eight. It covers Seoul's subway and Seoul city buses (night buses included) and, since 1 October 2026, getting off at Incheon Airport. It does not cover the Shinbundang Line, GTX, red wide-area buses, airport buses or stations outside Seoul. A heavy sightseeing day pays; a slow café day does not. Our call: worth it at three or more rides a day for three or more days inside Seoul.

## The subway in three taps

The subway is the backbone; the only rule that costs money is the exit tap.

1. **Tap in** at the gate. The reader shows your balance.
2. **Follow the line number and colour.** Changing lines inside a station needs no extra gate.
3. **Tap out** at the exit. The fare is worked out by distance.

{{fig:getting-around-3|Tap in, change lines inside, tap out}}

Fares (adult, since 28 June 2025): **₩1,550** by card for the first 10 km, then ₩100 every 5 km up to 50 km and every 8 km beyond. A single-journey ticket is ₩1,650 plus a ₩500 deposit you get back at the exit. Your first ride before 6:30 a.m. is 20% off with a card. Repeat visitor? The base fare was ₩1,400 before mid-2025.

For routes, use Naver Map or Kakao Map; Subway Korea works offline. Exits, transfers and first-day mistakes: [Seoul subway for first-timers](/seoul-subway/).

## Buses: tap off, every time

Buses are easy once you follow two habits: a loaded card when you board, a second tap when you get off.

{{fig:getting-around-4|On a bus you tap twice; the transfer clock starts when you tap off}}

1. **Tap your card** on the reader as you board.
2. **Tap it again** before you get off. Seoul says that without this tap you can't get a transfer, which means paying a full fare again on the next bus or subway.
3. **Board the next bus or subway within 30 minutes** (60 minutes between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m.) and it counts as a transfer: one base fare for the first 10 km, ₩100 per 5 km after that, up to four transfers. Re-boarding the same route does not count.

> Cash is not a backup. Seoul lists 183 of its 396 city bus routes as cashless (January 2026). Seoul's backup for people without a card is paying later by bank transfer, which doesn't help most visitors. Never board a bus without a loaded card.

| Colour | Type | Adult fare (card) |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | Trunk: long routes across the city | ₩1,500 |
| Green | Feeder and village buses: short hops to stations | ₩1,500 (village ₩1,200) |
| Yellow | Circular: loops in the centre | ₩1,400 |
| Red | Express to the suburbs | ₩3,000 |
| "N" number | Night bus | ₩2,500 |

Bus colours and night buses in detail: [Seoul city buses](/seoul-bus/).

## After the last train

Decide how you get back before dinner, not at the platform.

{{fig:getting-around-5|After dinner: how to get back}}

- **Last trains:** in 2022 Seoul set weekday last trains to reach the end of the line around 1 a.m. In May 2025 it announced moving Lines 1–8 first and last trains 30 minutes earlier; we could not confirm that this has happened. Times also differ by station, so check yours in Naver Map.
- **Night buses:** Seoul runs **14 routes** whose numbers start with N, between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m., for ₩2,500 by card. The Climate Card covers them. Around holidays Seoul sometimes extends last trains; it did for Lunar New Year 2026.

## Taxis

A taxi is the plan B, and the price of plan B depends on the clock.

{{fig:getting-around-6|Regular taxi: base fare by time of night}}

- **Regular (mid-size):** ₩4,800 for the first 1.6 km, then ₩100 per 131 m, or per 30 seconds in slow traffic.
- **Late night, regular taxis:** +20% from 10 to 11 p.m. and 2 to 4 a.m. (base ₩5,800), +40% from 11 p.m. to 2 a.m. (base ₩6,700).
- **Deluxe (mobeom) and large:** ₩7,000 for the first 3 km; +20% from 10 p.m. to 4 a.m.
- **International Taxi (for foreign visitors):** the meter plus up to 20%, or fixed fares from Incheon Airport (₩70,000–95,000 by Seoul zone for mid-size; [airport page](/airport-to-seoul/)).

**Which app?** Kakao Mobility makes two. **k.ride** is built for visitors: sign up by verifying your phone or with a social media account, register a credit or debit card (Alipay+ and WeChat Pay also work), and the fare is charged automatically when the ride ends. You can search for destinations and message drivers in 133 languages; the app itself is in English, Japanese, Simplified and Traditional Chinese. In Korea it calls Kakao T Blue, Venti, Black and deluxe taxis. Kakao T is the main Korean app; if your foreign card keeps failing there, switch to k.ride.

{{fig:getting-around-7|How k.ride works for visitors}}

Who doesn't need a taxi app: if you are in bed by 11 p.m. and travel light, the subway covers you.
