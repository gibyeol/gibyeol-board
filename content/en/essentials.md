## The checklist

| What | Do it when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| e-Arrival Card | Within 72 hours before you land | Mandatory for most foreign visitors since 1 January 2026 |
| K-ETA | Before booking, only if your country needs it | Most short-stay visitors from 22 countries and regions are exempt until 31 December 2026 |
| Mobile data | Before you fly | Maps, taxis and translation all need it from the airport onwards |
| Transport card | At the airport or first convenience store | Subway and bus fares are tap-only |
| Map and taxi apps | At home, on wifi | Google Maps still cannot give full directions in Korea |
| Some cash | Before or on arrival | Topping up a transport card usually means cash |

## 1. e-Arrival Card: now mandatory

Since 1 January 2026, foreign nationals entering Korea submit an electronic arrival card online within the three days before arrival. Paper cards are no longer the default.

- Apply only on the official site, [e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr). Look-alike sites charge a fee for the same form.
- You need your passport photo page, flight number and where you are staying.
- You get a confirmation PDF. It is valid for 72 hours, so if your flight moves by more than that, submit again.
- You do not need one if you hold a valid K-ETA or are a registered foreign resident.

## 2. K-ETA: check whether you are exempt

K-ETA is Korea's electronic travel authorization for visa-free visitors. It normally costs ₩10,000 and is valid for up to three years.

Korea has extended the temporary K-ETA exemption to **31 December 2026**. It covers 22 countries and regions, including the United States, Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. When you enter your nationality on [k-eta.go.kr](https://www.k-eta.go.kr), an exempt passport shows a pop-up saying so.

> Travelling in 2027? On 23–24 November 2026, travel media reported that the Ministry of Justice has extended the exemption to 31 December 2027. When we checked on 25 November, the official K-ETA site had not posted the notice yet, so treat it as reported, not confirmed. We will update this page as soon as it is official.

Exempt travellers can still apply for a K-ETA voluntarily. The only benefit is skipping the e-Arrival Card, and the fee applies.

## 3. Mobile data: eSIM is the easy option

Almost everything you will do in Korea runs through your phone: maps, taxis, translation, menus with QR codes. You have three choices:

- **eSIM.** Installs before you fly and works when you land. Check first that your phone supports eSIM and is carrier-unlocked.
- **Physical SIM.** Picked up at the airport. Useful if your phone has no eSIM.
- **Pocket wifi.** Good for groups sharing one connection, but it is one more thing to charge and return.

## 4. Money and transport cards

Credit cards are accepted almost everywhere in shops and restaurants. Where you still need cash is the transport card.

- **T-money** is the standard tap card for subway, bus and taxis across Korea. You buy it and top it up at convenience stores and station machines, and topping up a regular T-money card still usually means cash.
- **WOWPASS** is a prepaid card for visitors that combines currency exchange, card payments and T-money in one. You load it at machines in major stations with your home currency.
- **Climate Card** is Seoul's unlimited transit pass, with short-term versions for visitors. It only works in Seoul, so skip it if you are mostly elsewhere.
- **New in 2026:** since 17 March, new ticket machines at 273 stations on Seoul subway Lines 1–8 take foreign Visa and Mastercard for Climate Card short-term passes and single tickets (about 3.7% fee). On iPhone, the MobileTmoney app can be topped up with a foreign Mastercard, Amex or UnionPay card (not Visa). Tapping your own bank card at the gate does not work yet; Seoul plans it for the subway from 2027.

## 5. Apps to download before you fly

Download these on wifi at home and set the language to English, Japanese or Chinese first.

| App | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Naver Map | Walking, subway and bus directions | The standard in Korea. Supports Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese |
| Kakao Map | Directions | A good second opinion; feels closest to Google Maps |
| Kakao T | Taxis | English interface. Pay by card or in the car |
| Papago | Translation | Point the camera at a menu or sign |
| Subway Korea | Subway routes and transfers | Works offline once downloaded |
| KorailTalk | KTX and intercity trains | Book seats ahead on weekends |

### What about Google Maps?

In February 2026 the Korean government conditionally approved Google's request to export detailed map data, and Google has said it plans full navigation in Korea, including turn-by-turn directions. In mid-November the government confirmed Google had met the security conditions, but no launch date has been given. Until it launches, use Naver Map for directions.

## 6. Getting from Incheon Airport

The airport railroad (AREX) and limousine buses run to central Seoul, and both take a T-money card. If you land late, arrive with heavy bags or travel as a group, a pre-booked transfer is often simpler.
