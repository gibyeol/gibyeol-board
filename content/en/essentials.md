## Your first hour, if these are done

The arrival card was filed at home and its confirmation is on your phone. The eSIM connects once you land, your hotel is already saved in Naver Map, and at the airport railroad station you tap a transport card at the gate. Nothing in that hour needs a form, a SIM counter or a currency exchange queue. That is the whole point of this page: do six small things at home, and the first scene of the trip is the ride into the city, not a queue.

## The checklist

| What | Do it when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| e-Arrival Card | Within 3 days before you land | Required for most foreign visitors since 1 January 2026 |
| K-ETA | Before booking, only if your passport needs it | The exemption for 22 countries and regions now runs to 31 December 2027 |
| Mobile data | Before you fly | Maps, taxis and translation all need it from the airport on |
| Transport card | At the airport or first convenience store | Subway and bus fares are tap-only |
| Map and taxi apps | At home, on wifi | Google Maps still cannot give full directions in Korea |
| Some cash | Before or on arrival | Topping up a regular T-money card needs cash, and some market food stalls do not take foreign cards ([Money in Korea](/money/)) |

How we chose: these are the six things that stop you on day one if they are missing. What we left out: travel insurance, power adapters and packing for the weather ([Seoul in winter](/winter/)), because none of them blocks your first hour. Before you go, also put the three emergency numbers in your phone ([Emergencies](/emergency/)).

## 1. e-Arrival Card: required for most visitors

Foreign visitors file an electronic arrival card online, free, from three days before arrival. Incheon Airport says all foreigners are required to submit it in principle; the official FAQ still allows filing online or on paper before immigration if you missed it, but don't plan on paper. Field-by-field help: [Korea e-Arrival Card](/e-arrival-card/).

- Apply only on the official site, [e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr). Look-alike sites charge a fee for the same form.
- You need your passport photo page, flight number and where you are staying.
- You get a confirmation PDF. It is valid for 72 hours, so if your flight moves by more than that, submit again.
- You do not need one if you hold a valid K-ETA or are a registered foreign resident.

## 2. K-ETA: check whether you are exempt

K-ETA is Korea's electronic travel authorization for visa-free visitors. It costs ₩10,000 and is valid for up to three years.

On 23 November 2026 the Ministry of Justice [posted on the K-ETA site](https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/board/viewboarddetail.do?bbsSn=332883) that the temporary exemption is extended by a year, to **31 December 2027**, for the countries already covered (22 countries and regions, including the United States, Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau). When you scan your passport photo page in the K-ETA application, an exempt passport shows a "K-ETA exempt" notice.

Exempt travellers can still apply voluntarily. The benefit is skipping the arrival card, and the fee applies. A K-ETA you already hold stays valid until it expires, and its fee is not refunded.

## 3. Mobile data: eSIM is the easy option

Maps, taxis, translation and QR-code menus all run through your phone. Our call: eSIM, unless your phone can't take one.

**Check your phone first:** an iPhone XS or XR or later works if **Settings → General → About → Carrier Lock** says "No SIM Restrictions". On a Galaxy, an EID under **Settings → About phone → Status** means it has eSIM (the EID is also printed on the box). If your Galaxy came from a carrier, ask that carrier whether it is locked. Exceptions (Apple): in China mainland only iPhone 18 Pro, 18 Pro Max, iPhone Duo, 17e and iPhone Air support eSIM, and in Hong Kong and Macao some iPhone models use Dual SIM with two nano-SIM cards. If your iPhone was bought there, check the model first.

<details class="tr-more"><summary><i class="ic ic-list-checks" aria-hidden="true"></i>After you buy: install and turn on the eSIM (iPhone and Galaxy)</summary>

Install it **before you fly, on wifi**. The QR code usually comes by email or in the seller's app (your order confirmation says how).

**iPhone** (Apple's own instructions)

1. **Install:** scan the QR code with the Camera app → tap the "Cellular Plan Detected" notification → tap Continue at the bottom → Add Cellular Plan. A QR code in an email or browser: touch and hold it, then tap Add eSIM. A link instead: tap it → Allow → Continue.
2. **Turn it on when you land:** if a "Turn On Travel eSIM" notification appears, tap it, choose your lines, tap Continue (iOS 26 and later). Otherwise go to **Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data** and pick the travel eSIM. Only one number uses data at a time.
3. **Turn on Data Roaming for the travel eSIM:** tap **Settings → Cellular → your travel eSIM number** and turn Data Roaming on. Data Roaming lets you use the internet over a cellular network outside your carrier's network. If the seller's instructions say otherwise, follow them.
4. **Avoid roaming charges on your home number:** with both lines on, your home line can still incur roaming fees. Tap **Settings → Cellular → your home number** and turn Data Roaming off. Ask your home carrier before you go what calls and texts will cost abroad.
5. **If it doesn't connect:** turn Airplane Mode on and off → in Settings → Cellular, turn that line off and on → restart. Still nothing: contact the seller (note the error message and your EID: Settings → General → About).

**Galaxy** (Samsung's instructions; menu names vary a little by model and software)

1. **Install:** **Settings → Connections → SIM manager → Add eSIM → Scan QR code**, then scan your code. Got a code instead: on the same screen tap Enter activation code.
2. **Turn it on when you land:** in **Settings → Connections → SIM manager**, switch the travel eSIM on and set Mobile data to the travel eSIM.
3. **No data?** Check **Settings → Connections → Mobile networks → Data roaming** is on. If it is greyed out, turn on mobile data first.
4. **Still nothing:** restart, then contact the seller.

## 4. Money and transport cards

In shops and restaurants, try your credit card first. Some places, such as market stalls, do not take foreign cards. Cash still matters for the transport card.

{{fig:essentials-4|Four cards, and what you can pay for them with}}

- **T-money** is the standard tap card for subway, bus and taxis across Korea. You buy and top it up at convenience stores and station machines, and topping up a regular card usually means won cash. How to buy one: [convenience stores](/convenience-store/#tmoney); how to top up: [Seoul subway, step 2](/seoul-subway/).
- **AREX T-money card (new, 23 November 2026):** a preloaded T-money card sold at the T-money machines in the airport railroad stations at Terminals 1 and 2. It costs ₩15,000 (₩5,000 card plus ₩10,000 credit) and the machines take foreign credit cards. AREX has not said whether you can reload it by card later ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10947683)). Where it fits on arrival: [Incheon Airport to Seoul, step 3](/airport-to-seoul/).
- **WOWPASS** combines currency exchange, card payments and T-money. You load it at machines in major stations with your home currency. The **T-money balance for subway and bus is separate**: top it up with cash at stations and convenience stores, or move WOWPASS balance into it in the app. Collecting, loading and getting leftover money back: [airport guide](/airport-to-seoul/#prep); who it suits: [Money in Korea](/money/).
- **Climate Card** is Seoul's unlimited pass, with short-term versions for visitors. Since 17 March 2026, new machines at 273 stations on Seoul subway Lines 1–8 sell these passes and single tickets to foreign Visa and Mastercard (about 3.7% fee). Seoul only, so skip it if you are mostly elsewhere. Does it pay off for you: [Seoul subway](/seoul-subway/).

On iPhone, the MobileTmoney app can be topped up with a foreign Mastercard, Amex or UnionPay card (not Visa). Tapping your own bank card at the gate does not work yet; Seoul plans it for the subway from 2027. Topping up in the app, step by step: [convenience store guide](/convenience-store/#tmoney).

You don't have to buy ahead: the convenience store and card machines on the airport's B1 sell them too. Where to collect a pre-ordered card and how to use it: [airport guide, 'Want it ready'](/airport-to-seoul/#prep).

## 5. Apps to download before you fly

Download these on wifi at home and set the language first. Naver Map is the one to learn; the rest are backups and single jobs.

{{fig:essentials-5|The six apps, and the one job each does}}

| App | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Naver Map | Walking, subway and bus directions | The standard in Korea. Supports Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese |
| Kakao Map | Directions | To compare with Naver Map |
| Kakao T | Taxis | If you have a Korean number and card. With a foreign card, use k.ride, the visitor app ([Getting around](/getting-around/)) |
| Papago | Translation | Point the camera at a menu or sign |
| Subway Korea | Subway routes and transfers | For checking the line map. Naver Map above also gives subway directions |
| Korail+ (formerly KorailTalk) | KTX and intercity trains | Tickets open two months before departure ([KTX to Busan](/ktx-busan/)) |

### What about Google Maps?

In February 2026 the government conditionally approved Google's request to export detailed map data, and Google has said it plans full navigation in Korea, including turn-by-turn directions. On 13 November it confirmed Google had met the security conditions, and on 23 November Google announced partnerships for transit, parking and charging data, but still no launch date for full directions ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10946522)). Until it launches, use Naver Map.

## 6. Getting from Incheon Airport

The airport railroad (AREX) and limousine buses run to central Seoul, and both take a T-money card. If your hotel is near Seoul Station, the Express train is fastest (43 minutes from Terminal 1). Buy the ticket at the machines on the airport's B1 (foreign cards work), or get it on your phone in advance with the button below. Three or four people with lots of luggage: see the flat-fare [international taxi](/airport-to-seoul/#taxi). The full comparison by hotel area is in [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/airport-to-seoul/).
