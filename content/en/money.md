## The short answer

| Where | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Department stores, restaurants, convenience stores, taxis | Your foreign card | Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB and UnionPay are widely accepted |
| Gwangjang or Namdaemun market food, street stalls | Cash | Some small stalls cannot take foreign cards |
| Subway and bus | A transport card | You can't tap a foreign bank card at the gate yet |
| Delivery and booking apps | Depends on the app | See "When your card is rejected" below |

Our call on cards: bring two from different networks (say, Visa plus Mastercard or JCB). If one is turned down, you can try the other brand.

## Your first day with money in Korea

**Before you fly.** Two cards from different networks in your wallet, and the apps you plan to use (Naver Map, a delivery app, k.ride) installed at home on wifi.

**First evening.** A little won from the airport counter gets you to the hotel. In the city you change the rest for ₩10,000 notes. Dinner and the convenience store go on your card; at the market stall, you hand over a note.

**Last day.** Coins spent at a convenience store, notes kept if you are coming back.

What we left out: a bank-by-bank comparison of foreign transaction fees (they change too often for us to confirm), and other prepaid visitor cards, whose fees we have not checked.

## Cash: about ₩100,000, changed in the city

Our call: about ₩100,000 covers a 3–5 day Seoul trip. Cash really only goes on market food, stalls and topping up a transport card. The mistake to avoid is changing everything at the airport counter on arrival.

{{fig:money-1|Where to change money, usually best to worst}}

1. **At the airport, change only enough to reach your hotel.**
2. **In the city, change the rest at a money changer.** They usually beat city banks, and banks usually beat the airport.
3. **Ask for ₩10,000 notes,** so market stalls can make change.
4. **Pay by card everywhere else.** Keep the cash for market food, stalls and transport-card top-ups.

> Withdrawing instead? Look for machines marked "Global ATM". Expect a machine fee (often ₩2,000 or more) on top of your own bank's fee, so take out larger amounts less often.

## When your card is rejected

Korean apps turning down foreign cards is usually not your card. Many shops sign domestic-only payment contracts. A few things got better in 2026, so first find your situation:

{{fig:money-2|Card turned down? What works now, by situation}}

- **Food delivery (Baemin):** since June, Apple Pay works with foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB and Amex; since July you can order with a foreign card and no Korean phone number.
- **Naver Map:** since June, passport verification unlocks bookings, ordering and payment.
- **Taxis:** if your card keeps failing, Kakao Mobility's app for visitors, k.ride, takes overseas cards.
- **Subway:** since 17 March, 440 new machines at 273 stations on Seoul Lines 1–8 take foreign cards for the Climate Card, including 1–7 day short-term passes. A 3.7% fee applies to foreign cards.

Anywhere else, try your second card, then cash.

## Is WOWPASS worth it?

It is a prepaid card for visitors that combines currency exchange, card payments and T-money. Worth it if you will change cash often; skip it if you will pay by card anyway.

{{fig:money-3|WOWPASS costs, from the official site}}

The card costs ₩6,000, or ₩5,500 if you reserve it in advance, as listed on wowpass.io on 26 November 2026. Paying in shops has no fee; cash top-ups (16 currencies at the machines) convert at WOWPASS's own posted rate (the official guide of 4 November 2025 listed no fee for loading foreign cash; the current site does not say). Withdrawals are won cash only, up to ₩100,000 at a time, with a ₩1,000 fee; coins and amounts under ₩1,000 can't be withdrawn.

## Leftover won

Spend your coins before you fly; changing won back at home costs you. A WOWPASS balance comes back as won in cash only.

> Coming back to Korea? Keep the notes for next time. It is the coins that are hard to use up later.
