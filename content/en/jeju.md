## Your first afternoon on Jeju

You walk out to the first floor of Jeju International Airport and find Platform 2, in front of Gate 2. Express bus 101 stops there, with a luggage compartment underneath; you tell the driver before your stop that you have a bag in it. There is no cash to count out: since October 2024 Jeju's city buses take only cards and QR payment. Ten stops and about 40 minutes later you are at Hamdeok Beach, the first stop on VisitKorea's own east-coast bus itinerary. From there the slower 201 follows the coast to Seongsan Ilchulbong, the volcanic tuff cone. Our pick for the ending scene: make Seongsan Ilchulbong the day's last stop and sleep on the east coast, rather than riding back across the island.

## The one-line judgment

| Situation | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Going from Seoul to Jeju | Fly from Gimpo, and book early | It is the busiest air route in the world, but seats on it fell in 2026 |
| Landing at Incheon and heading straight to Jeju | Check Jeju Air's Incheon–Jeju flight (Mon, Wed, Fri) first; otherwise ride to Gimpo | It saves the cross-town transfer, but it only flies three days a week |
| No car | Sleep in Jeju City or on the east coast, ride buses, add taxis | Bus 101 and bus 201 connect the airport and the east coast; cash is not accepted on board |
| Want to drive | Get an international driving permit at home before you fly | Korea lets you drive on it for one year from entry, only for the vehicle classes on it |

How we chose: one base, the two bus lines that link the airport to the east coast, and one decision about the car. What we left out: the west coast and the inland roads, which we have not checked by bus, and rental-car companies. Who doesn't need this page: if you have three days in Korea, skip Jeju. The flight is short, but airport time on both ends eats most of a day.

## Getting there: Gimpo, not Incheon

Gimpo–Jeju is the world's busiest airline route. Aviation data firm OAG counted about 14.4 million scheduled seats on it in 2025, and Korean press, citing IATA, put 2025 passengers at 13.3 million. Busy does not mean easy to book: in June 2026 Jeju–Gimpo flights were down by 343 from a year earlier, about 11 fewer a day, partly because slots moved to low-cost airlines flying smaller planes, and domestic seats at Jeju Airport fell 9.1% that month. Our take: buy your Jeju ticket when you book your Seoul hotel, not the week before.

{{fig:jeju-1|Incheon to Jeju: through Gimpo, or direct on three days a week}}

Gimpo is on the AREX line from Incheon, so the [Incheon to Seoul guide](/airport-to-seoul/) covers getting there. Jeju Air flies Incheon–Jeju on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays from 2 November 2026 and has extended the route through 27 March 2027.

> Bring your passport to the domestic flight. Korean Air lists it as accepted ID for foreign adults and closes check-in 30 minutes before departure (boarding closes 10 minutes before). Low-cost airlines can be stricter, so check yours.

## Getting around without a car

You can do Jeju without a car if you pick your base and your sights around two bus lines.

{{fig:jeju-2|Bus 101 and bus 201 on the east side of Jeju}}

- **Express bus 101** leaves the airport (Platform 2, first floor) and runs east along the north coast to Namwon in Seogwipo, with few stops. Fare from ₩2,000 by card; it varies with distance.
- **Bus 201** runs from Jeju City Bus Terminal along the east coast to Seogwipo, stopping at almost every stop, including near Seongsan Ilchulbong. It does not go to the airport and runs every 30 to 80 minutes. Fare ₩1,150 by card.
- **Airport limousine buses** 600 and 800 also leave from the airport (Jeju Regional Aviation Office). The airport's guide lists the 600 running to Seogwipo, but that guide dates from 2020, so confirm at the stop.

Paying on board, since cash stopped on 1 October 2024:

1. Before you board, have a loaded Korean transport card, or a payment app that supports the bus QR code.
2. Paying by QR: find the code on the pole by the driver and scan it. The instructions next to it are in several languages.
3. Enter the fare: ₩1,150 on a regular bus, ₩2,000 on the express 101.

{{fig:jeju-3|Paying on a Jeju bus: card or QR, no cash}}

Those fares were last updated by VisitKorea in January 2025; Korean press still quoted ₩1,150 in May 2026. What buses do not solve: gaps of up to an hour and anything inland. Budget one or two taxi rides a day instead of waiting for a bus that comes once an hour. Taxi apps are in [Getting around](/getting-around/).

## Driving as a tourist

Your home licence on its own does not let you drive. Korea lets holders of an international driving permit drive for **one year from the date of entry**, and only the vehicle classes written on the permit. Get it at home before you fly. Japanese and Taiwanese readers have country-specific notes in their editions.

## Where to stay

| Area | Good for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Jeju City | Short stays, late arrivals, bus connections | The least scenic, and cooler than the south |
| East coast (Hamdeok to Seongsan) | Car-free trips: bus 101 from the airport, bus 201 along the coast | Fewer evening options; buses can be 30–80 minutes apart |
| Seogwipo (south) | Warmer winters, south coast sights, the airport limousine | Furthest from the airport |

## Weather, briefly

Jeju is the mild corner of Korea in winter, and the south side is milder still. In winter 2025 (December 2025 to February 2026) the Korea Meteorological Administration recorded an average daily high of 13.6°C in Seogwipo and 11.2°C in Jeju City, both among the warmest on record; that winter was also unusually dry. Pack a windproof layer anyway. For the rest of the country, see our [winter guide](/winter/).

## The Jeju visa-free rule

Under Jeju's special law, eligible foreigners can enter Jeju for tourism or transit without a visa and stay 30 days. Nationals on a list set by Korea's Justice Minister are excluded, travel on to the mainland may need separate permission, and it does not skip immigration checks.

A US, Japanese or Taiwanese passport already gets you into all of Korea visa-free for short trips, so this rule changes nothing for you. What does matter: Korea's temporary K-ETA exemption, for the countries it already covers, now runs to 31 December 2027 (Justice Ministry notice, 23 November 2026). Scanning your passport on the K-ETA site tells you whether you are exempt; if you are, you file the free [e-Arrival Card](/e-arrival-card/) instead.

On 13 November 2026 a lawmaker introduced a bill to repeal the Jeju visa-free articles. Jeju's governor opposes it, and as of 18 November it still needed National Assembly review. The current rule remains in effect.

## Before you go

1. Book the Gimpo–Jeju flight the day you book your Seoul hotel. If you land at Incheon on a Monday, Wednesday or Friday, check the direct flight first.
2. Put a loaded transport card in your wallet; Jeju's buses will not take cash.
3. Driving? Get the international permit at home.
4. The evening before, trace bus 201 on Naver Map: Jeju City, Hamdeok, Seongsan Ilchulbong, Seogwipo. It is the east coast in one line, and your first afternoon.
