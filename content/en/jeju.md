## The one-line judgment

| Situation | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Going from Seoul to Jeju | Fly from Gimpo, and book early | It is the busiest air route in the world, but seats on it fell in 2026 |
| Landing at Incheon and heading straight to Jeju | Check Jeju Air's Incheon–Jeju flight (Mon, Wed, Fri) first; otherwise ride to Gimpo | It saves the cross-town transfer, but it only flies three days a week |
| No car | Sleep in Jeju City or on the east coast, ride buses, add taxis | The airport express bus and the east coast bus line connect the sights; cash is not accepted on board |
| Want to drive | Get an international driving permit at home before you fly | Korea lets you drive on it for one year from entry, only for the vehicle classes on it |
| US, Japanese or Taiwanese passport | Same entry rules as the rest of Korea | Jeju's own visa-free rule is for nationals who would otherwise need a visa |

Who doesn't need this page: if you have three days in Korea, skip Jeju. The flight is short, but airport time on both ends eats most of a day.

## Getting there: Gimpo, not Incheon

Gimpo–Jeju is the world's busiest airline route. Aviation data firm OAG counted about 14.4 million scheduled seats on it in 2025, and Korean press, citing IATA, put 2025 passengers at 13.3 million.

Busy does not mean easy to book. In June 2026, Jeju–Gimpo flights were down by 343 from a year earlier, about 11 fewer a day, partly because slots moved to low-cost airlines flying smaller planes. Domestic seats at Jeju Airport fell 9.1% that month. Our take: buy your ticket when you book your Seoul hotel, not the week before.

Gimpo is on the AREX line from Incheon, so the [Incheon to Seoul guide](/airport-to-seoul/) covers getting there.

**From Incheon directly:** Jeju Air flies Incheon–Jeju on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays from 2 November 2026, and has extended the route through 27 March 2027. If your international flight lands on one of those days, it can save you the trip across Seoul.

**At the airport:** bring your passport. Korean Air lists a passport as accepted ID for foreign adults on domestic flights, and closes check-in 30 minutes before departure (boarding closes 10 minutes before). Low-cost airlines can be stricter, so check yours.

## Getting around without a car

You can do Jeju without a car if you pick your base and your sights around the bus lines. The parts that work:

- **Express bus 101** leaves Jeju airport (Platform 2, in front of Gate 2 on the first floor) and runs east along the north coast to Namwon in Seogwipo, with few stops. Visit Korea's itinerary uses it to reach Hamdeok Beach in about 40 minutes.
- **Bus 201** runs from Jeju City Bus Terminal to Seogwipo along the east coast and stops near Seongsan Ilchulbong and other east coast sights. It does not go to the airport. It runs every 30 to 80 minutes, so check the time before you walk to the stop.
- **Airport limousine buses** (routes 600 and 800 are listed by the Jeju Regional Aviation Office) leave from the airport. The airport's own guide lists the 600 running to Seogwipo, but that guide dates from 2020, so confirm the route at the stop.
- **Fares:** Visit Korea lists ₩1,150 for a regular bus and from ₩2,000 for an express bus, by card or QR payment. **Cash has not been accepted on Jeju city buses since 1 October 2024.** Have a Korean transport card ready, or use the QR code on the pole by the driver with a supported payment app. Those fares were last updated in January 2025; Korean press still quoted ₩1,150 in May 2026.

What buses do not solve: gaps of up to an hour or more, and anything inland. Plan one or two taxi rides a day into your budget instead of forcing a bus that comes once an hour. Taxi apps are covered in [Getting around](/getting-around/).

## Driving as a tourist

Your home licence on its own is not what lets you drive. Korea lets holders of an international driving permit drive for **one year from the date of entry**, and only the vehicle classes written on the permit. Get the permit at home before you fly.

Japanese and Taiwanese readers have country-specific details in their editions of this page.

## Where to stay

| Area | Good for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Jeju City | Short stays, late arrivals, bus connections | The least scenic, and cooler than the south |
| East coast (Hamdeok to Seongsan) | Car-free trips: bus 101 from the airport, bus 201 along the coast | Fewer evening options; buses can be 30–80 minutes apart |
| Seogwipo (south) | Warmer winters, south coast sights, the airport limousine | Furthest from the airport |

We left out the west coast. We did not check its bus routes for this page, so we cannot recommend it without a car.

## Weather, briefly

Jeju is the mild corner of Korea in winter, and the south side is milder still. In winter 2025 (December 2025 to February 2026), the Korea Meteorological Administration recorded an average daily high of 13.6°C in Seogwipo and 11.2°C in Jeju City; both were among the warmest winters on record. That winter was also unusually dry. Pack a windproof layer either way. For the rest of the country in the cold months, see our [winter guide](/winter/).

## The Jeju visa-free rule

You will see "Jeju is visa-free" in a lot of articles. Here is what it means. Under Jeju's special law, eligible foreigners can enter Jeju for tourism or transit without a visa and stay 30 days. Nationals on a list set by Korea's Justice Minister are excluded, and travel on to the mainland may need separate permission. It does not skip immigration checks.

If you hold a US, Japanese or Taiwanese passport, you already enter all of Korea without a visa for short trips, so this rule changes nothing for you. Check [K-ETA and the e-Arrival Card](/essentials/) as you would for Seoul.

One thing to watch: on 13 November 2026, a lawmaker introduced a bill to repeal the Jeju visa-free articles. Jeju's governor opposes it, and as of 18 November it still needed National Assembly review. The current rule remains in effect.
