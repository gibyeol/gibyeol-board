## A BBQ evening

Here is the evening we would plan, built from the Korea Tourism Organization's (KTO) guide to Korean restaurants. At a barbecue place the charcoal grill may be built into the table. If your chair has a lid, lift it: storage chairs are there so your coat doesn't take on the smell of the food. Spoons, chopsticks and napkins are in a small drawer on the table, and there is a call button for staff, which KTO warns may be louder than you expect.

At some places staff grill the meat for you, and when they say it's ready you wrap a piece in a leaf (ssam) and eat. Want more? Call out "Yeogiyo!" (Excuse me!). Our pick for the ending is the one KTO names: a soybean-paste stew (doenjang jjigae) or fried rice to finish.

## Our call

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quick lunch, maybe alone | Kimbap shop, a stew with rice, a noodle or rice-soup place | Fast, single servings, no one minds a solo diner |
| BBQ night | Go with at least one other person, or ask "1인분 돼요?" at the door | Meat grilled at the table is often sold two servings minimum |
| Late night at the hotel | Order on Baemin | Foreign cards work and no Korean phone number is needed |
| Vegetarian, halal or allergic | Show a Korean sentence before you order | Broths, fish sauce and salted shrimp hide in dishes that look safe |

Our three: **Naver Map** for bookings, **Baemin** for nights in, and the **phrase card** at the bottom if you have a dietary limit. What we left out: restaurant rankings, and the booking apps we could not confirm for visitors. Who doesn't need this page? Someone who eats everything and is happy to walk in and point. For tipping (don't) and the two-serving rule in detail, see [Everyday Korea](/everyday/).

## What to eat, by situation

The dishes come from KTO's food pages, which also say what each one "usually has" in it. That note matters more than the description if you avoid something.

**Quick lunch.** Kimbap (rice and vegetables rolled in seaweed, usually with ham, egg or tuna) and kimchi jjigae (kimchi stew with tofu, usually with pork or tuna) are the everyday standbys. Noodle and rice-soup shops are the other easy solo choice.

**BBQ night.** Samgyeopsal is grilled pork belly. Dakgalbi, spicy chicken stir-fried with cabbage, sweet potato and rice cakes on a big pan, works the same way. Budae jjigae ("army stew" with sausage and ham) is one big pot for the table.

**Market and street food.** Tteokbokki (rice cakes in sweet-spicy sauce, usually with fish cake), eomuk (fish cake skewers in broth), hotteok (pancake with brown sugar, cinnamon and nuts) and bungeoppang (fish-shaped pastry with red bean; no fish in it). Small stalls often can't take foreign cards, so carry small notes; see [Money in Korea](/money/).

**Late night.** Delivery (below) or a [convenience store](/convenience-store/).

## How ordering works

More and more restaurants have you order, and often pay, on a tabletop tablet or a kiosk at the door (Korea Herald, January 2024; KTO). Find out which kind of place you are in, and the rest follows.

{{fig:food-2|Three ways to order and pay}}

- **Tablet or kiosk:** if the screen is Korean only, point Papago's camera at it.
- **Staff:** press the call button when you need someone.
- **Water and side dishes are free** to refill, says KTO; some places have a self-service corner.
- **Paying:** with a tablet you usually pay as you order. Otherwise take the slip to the counter on your way out ([Everyday Korea](/everyday/)).

## Reservations and queues: Naver Map

Since 4 June 2026, Naver has offered passport verification for visitors. With a passport issued outside Korea you verify on your phone, without a Korean number or a call to support, and that unlocks bookings, orders and payments in Naver Map. Each restaurant chooses whether to accept bookings and payments made with overseas-issued cards (Korea Herald, July 2026), so not every place will take yours.

{{fig:food-3|Booking a table in Naver Map as a visitor}}

What we could not confirm: whether Naver's waiting list is included, and whether CatchTable or Tabling work for visitors. If a place has a paper list or a tablet at the door, put your name down and wait nearby.

## Delivery for visitors: Baemin

Baemin (Baedal Minjok) is the delivery app that changed most for visitors in 2026:

- **Language:** English, Chinese and Japanese since February, following your phone's language.
- **Cards:** Apple Pay with foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB and Amex since 2 June; foreign credit cards, WeChat Pay and Alipay+ too.
- **No Korean phone:** since late July checkout no longer needs a Korean mobile subscription; you verify a text sent to your overseas number.

{{fig:food-4|Ordering on Baemin from your hotel}}

Our tip: copy your hotel's Korean address from Naver Map before you order, and ask the front desk where riders hand over food.

## Vegetarian and vegan

Assume a dish is not vegetarian until you ask. KTO's tips list the traps: kimchi often contains salted seafood or fish sauce; soft tofu stew and braised tofu usually include salted shrimp; bibimbap often comes with meat and egg; vegetable kimbap may still have ham, egg or crab stick. Safer bets KTO suggests: vegetable kimbap (check it), acorn jelly salad (dotori muk muchim), tofu, and temple food, made with no meat. Read [KTO's tips for vegetarians](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140732).

## Halal

KTO's [Muslim-friendly travel page](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/thingsToDo/subMuslimFriendly.do) sorts restaurants into four levels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Halal certified | Certified by a body such as the Korea Muslim Federation |
| Self-certified | All food halal, certified by the Muslim owner |
| Muslim friendly | Some halal dishes; alcohol may be sold |
| Pork free | No halal menu, no pork; alcohol may be sold |

KTO warns that hotel buffets labelled Muslim-friendly may still serve dishes with pork.

## Allergies

Korean law requires allergen labels only from certain chains: since July 2021, brands with 50 or more outlets selling foods like burgers, pizza, bakery goods and ice cream must show 22 allergens such as egg, milk, buckwheat, peanut, soy, wheat, shrimp, crab and pork. Small restaurants have no such list. If your allergy is severe, eat where ingredients are written down, and carry your medication.

### Phrase card (show it, don't read it)

| English | Korean |
|---|---|
| I'm allergic to ___. | 저는 ___ 알레르기가 있어요. |
| Is there ___ in this? | 이 음식에 ___ 들어가요? |
| Please leave out ___. | ___ 빼 주세요. |
| I'm vegetarian. / I'm vegan. | 저는 채식주의자예요. / 저는 비건이에요. |
| I don't eat meat, fish, eggs or milk. | 고기, 생선, 달걀, 우유를 안 먹어요. |
| Is the broth made with meat or anchovy? | 국물에 고기나 멸치가 들어가요? |
| Is there pork in this? | 돼지고기 들어가요? |
| Peanut / tree nuts / shrimp / crab / shellfish / egg / milk / wheat / buckwheat / soy | 땅콩 / 견과류 / 새우 / 게 / 조개류 / 달걀 / 우유 / 밀 / 메밀 / 콩 |

Before you fly, screenshot this card so it opens without data. For anything more complicated, the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline interprets by phone; see [Everyday Korea](/everyday/).
