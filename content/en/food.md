## Our call

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quick lunch, maybe alone | Kimbap shop, a stew with rice, a noodle or rice-soup place | Fast, single servings, no one minds a solo diner |
| BBQ night | Go with at least one other person, or ask "1인분 돼요?" at the door | Meat grilled at the table is often sold two servings minimum |
| Market or street stall | Bring cash, point and pay | Small stalls often can't take foreign cards |
| Late night at the hotel | Order on Baemin | It takes foreign cards and no longer needs a Korean phone number |
| A popular restaurant you don't want to queue for | Book it in Naver Map after passport verification | Since June 2026 visitors can book, order and pay there |
| Vegetarian, halal or allergic | Show a Korean sentence before you order | Broths, fish sauce and salted shrimp hide in dishes that look safe |

## What to eat, by situation

The dishes below come from VisitKorea, the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) site, which also says what each one "usually has" in it. That note matters more than the description if you avoid something.

**Quick lunch.** Kimbap (rice and vegetables rolled in seaweed, usually with ham, egg or tuna) and kimchi jjigae (kimchi stew with tofu and rice, usually with pork or tuna) are the everyday standbys. Noodle and rice-soup shops are the other easy solo choice.

**BBQ night.** Samgyeopsal is grilled pork belly, cooked at your table and eaten wrapped in leaves (ssam) with side dishes. KTO says you can just call out "Yeogiyo!" (Excuse me!) when you want more. Dakgalbi, spicy chicken stir-fried with cabbage, sweet potato and rice cakes on a big pan at the table, works the same way. Both are sharing food; budae jjigae ("army stew" with sausage and ham) is one big pot for the table.

**Market and street food.** Tteokbokki (rice cakes in sweet-spicy sauce, usually with fish cake), eomuk (fish cake skewers in broth), twigim (fried vegetables, sweet potato or squid; dip it in the tteokbokki sauce), hotteok (pancake with brown sugar, cinnamon and nuts), gyeranppang (bread baked with a whole egg) and bungeoppang (fish-shaped pastry with red bean; no fish in it). Carry small notes; see [Money in Korea](/money/).

**Late night.** Delivery is the local answer (below). The other is a convenience store; see our [convenience store guide](/convenience-store/).

**Cafés.** Expect to order at the counter or a kiosk. Nothing special to learn beyond the kiosk tips below.

## How ordering works

- **Tablet or kiosk.** Many restaurants now ask you to order, and often pay up front, on a tabletop tablet or a kiosk at the door (Korea Herald, January 2024). If the screen is Korean only, point Papago's camera at it.
- **Call bell.** If you ordered from staff, press the button on the table when you need someone. Every table has one (KTO).
- **Water and side dishes are free.** KTO says asking for more water or banchan costs nothing. Some places have a self-service corner where you refill them yourself.
- **Spoon and chopsticks** are in a drawer under the table. Using your spoon when chopsticks get tricky is fine (KTO).
- **Paying.** With a tablet you usually pay as you order. Otherwise take the slip to the counter on your way out; see [Everyday Korea](/everyday/).

## Reservations and queues: Naver Map

Since 4 June 2026, Naver has offered passport verification for visitors (announced 9 June). With a passport issued outside Korea you verify on your phone, without a Korean number or a call to customer support, and that unlocks bookings, orders and payments through Naver Map. Naver says it will add services in stages, so not every restaurant or feature will be covered.

What we could not confirm: whether Naver's waiting-list feature is included, and whether CatchTable or Tabling (other Korean booking and queue apps) work for visitors. We left them out rather than guess. If a place has a paper list or a tablet at the door, put your name down and wait nearby.

## Delivery for visitors: Baemin

Baemin (Baedal Minjok) is the delivery app that has done the most for visitors in 2026:

- **Language:** English, Chinese and Japanese since February. The app follows your phone's language; translation is AI-based and covers search, menus, payment and delivery status.
- **Cards:** Apple Pay with foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB and Amex since 2 June. Baemin also takes foreign credit cards, WeChat Pay and Alipay+.
- **No Korean phone:** since late July you can order without signing up, by verifying a text message sent to your overseas number.

Our tip: copy your hotel's Korean address from Naver Map before you order, and check with the front desk where riders hand over food.

## Vegetarian and vegan

Assume a dish is not vegetarian until you ask. KTO's vegetarian tips list the traps: kimchi often contains salted seafood or fish sauce; soft tofu stew and braised tofu usually include salted shrimp; bibimbap often comes with meat and egg; vegetable kimbap may still have ham, egg or crab stick; pancake batter can contain egg. Safer bets KTO suggests: vegetable kimbap (check it), acorn jelly salad (dotori muk muchim), tofu, and temple food, which is made with no meat. KTO also says vegetarian restaurants, bakeries and cafés are increasing. Read [KTO's tips for vegetarians](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140732).

## Halal

KTO's [Muslim-friendly travel page](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/thingsToDo/subMuslimFriendly.do) sorts restaurants into four levels, and the difference matters:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Halal certified | Certified by a body such as the Korea Muslim Federation |
| Self-certified | All food halal, certified by the Muslim owner |
| Muslim friendly | Some halal dishes; alcohol may be sold |
| Pork free | No halal menu, no pork; alcohol may be sold |

KTO warns that hotel buffets labelled Muslim-friendly may still serve dishes with pork. The same page links its Muslim-friendly restaurant e-book (2022 edition).

## Allergies

Korean law requires allergen labelling only from certain chains: since July 2021, brands with 50 or more outlets selling foods like burgers, pizza, bakery goods and ice cream must show 22 allergens such as egg, milk, buckwheat, peanut, soy, wheat, shrimp, crab and pork. Small restaurants have no such list, and the person taking your order may not know every ingredient. If your allergy is severe, our honest advice is to eat where the ingredients are written down, and carry your medication.

### Phrase card (show it, don't read it)

| English | Korean |
|---|---|
| I'm allergic to ___. | 저는 ___ 알레르기가 있어요. |
| Is there ___ in this? | 이 음식에 ___ 들어가요? |
| Please leave out ___. | ___ 빼 주세요. |
| I'm vegetarian. / I'm vegan. | 저는 채식주의자예요. / 저는 비건이에요. |
| I don't eat meat, fish, eggs or milk. | 고기, 생선, 달걀, 우유를 안 먹어요. |
| Is the broth made with meat or anchovy? | 국물에 고기나 멸치가 들어가요? |
| Is there pork in this? | 돼지고기 들어가요? |
| Peanut / tree nuts / shrimp / crab / shellfish / egg / milk / wheat / buckwheat / soy | 땅콩 / 견과류 / 새우 / 게 / 조개류 / 달걀 / 우유 / 밀 / 메밀 / 콩 |

For anything more complicated, the 1330 Korea Travel Hotline interprets by phone; details on [Everyday Korea](/everyday/).
