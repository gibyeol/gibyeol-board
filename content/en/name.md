## How it works

- **Sound.** Your name in Korean, Japanese and the characters Taiwan uses. Korean names also get the official romanization and an approximate katakana.
- **Meaning.** Where the name comes from and what it means, marked well documented, likely or uncertain, with the source. Then the meaning as a word and as a real name in Korean, Japanese and Chinese: Grace becomes 은혜, 恵(めぐみ) and 恩惠.
- **Your name in other countries.** Sophia is Sofía in Spain, Zofia in Poland, Zsófia in Hungary. Variants like Yusuf or Seán lead back to their root name.
- **Where it is most loved.** Where the name ranks highest among baby names in official statistics. These are baby-name ranks, not counts of people.
- **Where it leads around the world.** For each meaning, a checked story or place in many countries, each with its source, the date we checked it and a confidence mark (legends are marked as legends). Korea is one of them, and its chain leads into our Korea guides. Then "If you were born in…": the name with the same meaning in each country, a space for one line from someone who went, and the next name to follow.
- **Chinese characters and kanji** are read the Korean way with the meaning of each character: 花 is "꽃 화", flower + hwa.
- **17 languages.** Switch the language at the top of the tool.

We add checked names every week. A name that isn't on the list still gets a Korean reading, marked as approximate. Speak a language we are missing, or know your name's story? The links under each result come straight to us.
