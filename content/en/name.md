## How it works

- **Sound.** Your name written in Korean, Japanese katakana and the characters Taiwan uses for it. Korean names also get the official romanization and an approximate katakana.
- **Meaning.** Where the name comes from and what it means, marked *well documented*, *likely* or *uncertain*, with a link to the source. Then the same meaning as a word, and as a real name, in Korean, Japanese and Chinese: Grace becomes 은혜, 恵(めぐみ) and 恩惠.
- **Chinese characters and kanji** are read the Korean way, one character at a time, with the way Koreans teach each one: 花 is "꽃 화", flower + hwa.
- **Where it leads.** Some names open a short chain into Korea: James is Santiago in Spanish, the Camino de Santiago inspired the Jeju Olle trail, and our Jeju guide is one tap away.

We start with the most common names and add more each week, only after checking the meaning. A name that isn't on the list still gets a Korean reading, marked as approximate. If anything looks wrong, the link under the result comes straight to us.
